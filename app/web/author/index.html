SEED GENERATION

What Generate Seed Needs

Generate Seed compiles one exact published World version with one exact published Adventure Brief version, then sends that locked source material through the configured generation provider.

REQUIREMENTS
  1. Open an Adventure Brief, not a World document.
  2. The Brief must be linked to a World.
  3. The linked World must have a published version.
  4. The currently selected Brief version must be published.
  5. The generation provider must be configured and available.

If you create a newer draft after publishing, that draft is not eligible until it is published. Generation always uses exact published versions so a seed can be traced back to its source.

BEGINNER GUIDE

How Authoring Works

You do not need to fill out everything. Give the system the ideas you care about and leave breathing room everywhere else. A few strong details can make a great adventure.
CREATE WORLD → PUBLISH → CREATE BRIEF → PUBLISH → GENERATE → REVIEW → APPROVE → PLAY
WORLD BIBLE

Define the playground.

Use a World Bible for facts that can matter across many adventures: places, recurring people, lore, tone, rules, secrets, and things the story should never contradict.

You can make it huge, but you do not have to. A premise, a few truths, a location or two, and a memorable NPC are enough to start.

ADVENTURE BRIEF

Define tonight's story.

A Brief points at a World and says what this particular adventure should be about: the hook, mood, length, difficulty, important characters or places, desired moments, and any special restrictions.

The Brief does not need to retell the whole World. It only needs to explain what matters for this run.

AUTHORITY LABELS

These tell the generator what it may improvise.

CANONTreat this as objectively true. Do not contradict it.
REQUIREDThis must be included or honored in the generated adventure.
PREFERREDTry to use this when it fits naturally, but the story may bend around it.
INSPIRATIONThis is creative fuel, not a promise. Remixing and improvisation are welcome.
FORBIDDENDo not include this or cross this boundary.
WHAT SHOULD I WORRY ABOUT?

Mostly the things you actually care about.

  • Be specific about non-negotiables. Put hard facts in Canon and hard boundaries in Forbidden Rules.
  • Do not chase 100% strength. More detail means more control, not automatically a better story.
  • Three words can be useful. An NPC can begin as “mean baker, secretly lonely.” You can always expand later.
  • Leave blanks on purpose. Empty space gives the generator room to surprise you.
  • Use Required sparingly. Too many mandatory beats can make a short adventure feel railroaded.
  • Secrets belong in the source. Players do not need to know them; the director can use them behind the scenes.
  • Write the feeling, not just the facts. Tone and Player Experience are useful when you care how the session should feel.
PUBLISHING & VERSIONS

Draft freely. Publish when it represents the truth you want used.

Drafts autosave and can keep changing. Publishing freezes that version so generation can point at an exact World version and exact Brief version. If you want to change a published source later, create a new version rather than rewriting history underneath an existing adventure.

THE IMPORTANT PART

You are not writing the whole adventure.

You are defining the sandbox, the boundaries, and the things worth caring about. The generator/director supplies connective tissue, scenes, surprises, complications, dialogue, and details inside those boundaries.

The game server still owns mechanical truth during play: character stats, checks, dice, inventory, flags, damage, and other canonical game state are not supposed to be invented by the narrator.
